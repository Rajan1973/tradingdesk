Quant Terminal user manual

A trading terminal that runs on your own computer, set up for Indian markets: NSE and BSE charts, Indian news, a Nifty 500 screener and heatmap, company financials, your own stock catalysts on the chart, and your Zerodha, Kotak Neo and ICICI Direct accounts (several of each). It also covers crypto, US stocks and forex.

Getting started

Opening the app

Quant Terminal runs in your web browser, but nothing is on the internet: the app itself runs on your computer. Its address is http://localhost:3777. Bookmark it.

Once started, it keeps running in the background, even after you close the browser tab or Claude. Just open the address again.

Starting and stopping the app yourself

You don't need Claude to run the app. In the quant-terminal folder:

  • Start Quant Terminal.bat: double-click to start the app and open it in your browser. It runs in the background and its window closes by itself. If the app is already running, it just opens the browser.
  • Stop Quant Terminal.bat: double-click to stop the app.

The app keeps running when you close the browser. It only stops when you use the stop file or restart your computer; after a restart, double-click the start file again.

Use these files, not start.batThe older start.bat ties the app to its window, and if the app is already running it starts a second copy on another port (3778), which your broker logins don't recognise.

Checking everything works

If something looks wrong, run the health check. It tests the app, checks which data sources your connection can reach, and ends with READY or NOT READY.

node scripts/check.js
Nothing to sign up forThe market data comes from free public sources. You only need keys if you connect a broker (see Broker).

Timeframes, chart types and layouts

Timeframes

Use the buttons (1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 1D, 1W) or type any timeframe into the box next to them: 7m, 2h, 3D, 2W, 3M. Press Enter.

Chart types

Candles, hollow candles, bars, Heikin Ashi, line, line with markers, step, area, baseline, columns, Renko, line break, range bars, Kagi, and point & figure.

Several charts at once

The layout button shows 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 or 16 charts. A common setup is one stock on 1D, 4h, 1h and 15m side by side.

  • Sync (top right checkbox) changes the symbol on every chart at once.
  • Crosshair across charts (in Settings) moves the crosshair on all charts together.

Zoom buttons

Move the mouse over a chart and a small bar appears at the bottom centre: zoom out, zoom in and reset. Zooming keeps the latest bar where it is, like TradingView. The bar hides while bar replay is running, because the replay controls sit in the same place. You can also zoom with the mouse wheel and drag the chart to scroll.

Reset a chart's view

If a chart gets hard to read after scrolling, zooming or dragging its price axis, click the reset button in the zoom bar, or right-click on it and choose Reset chart view. It goes back to the latest bars, with every price scale fitting the candles again and indicator panes at their usual height. Reset all chart views does the same for every chart in the layout.

This happens automatically whenever you change the instrument, whether by typing a symbol or clicking a watchlist row, screener result, heatmap tile, calendar entry or broker holding: every chart starts from a clean view of the new stock.

Countdown to the next bar

On intraday charts, a small timer under the last-price label shows how long until the current candle closes. It hides when the market is closed (after 15:30 IST for NSE and BSE), in bar replay, and on daily and longer charts.

Other chart tools

  • Compare overlays another symbol, for example a stock against the Nifty.
  • Log scale for long-term charts.
  • Download chart image saves a picture of the chart.
  • Export chart data saves the bars and every indicator's values as a CSV file for Excel.
  • Light / dark switches the colour theme.

Indicators

Click Indicators in the toolbar and search: 87 built in, with no limit per chart. They include moving averages, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, Supertrend, Ichimoku, VWAP and anchored VWAP, ADX, pivots, volume profiles and candlestick patterns.

  • Change settings: click the gear next to the indicator in the chart legend.
  • Hide or remove: the eye and × buttons in the legend.
  • Fold the indicator list: click the small ⌃ under a chart's indicators to fold the list into one chip such as ⌄ 6, so it doesn't cover the candles. Click the chip to unfold it. The indicators stay on the chart, and each chart remembers its own choice.
  • Indicator on an indicator: set an indicator's source to another indicator, for example an average of RSI.
  • Higher timeframe values: show, say, daily RSI on a 1h chart.

Drawing tools

The left toolbar has 13 tools: trend line, ray, extended line, horizontal line and ray, vertical line, parallel channel, rectangle, Fibonacci retracement, measure, long position, short position, and text.

  1. Pick a tool, then click on the chart for each point.
  2. The tool switches back to the cursor after each drawing. To keep drawing with the same tool, change After placing a drawing in Settings.
  3. Click a drawing to select it; drag its points to adjust it.
  4. Delete a selected drawing with Delete or the bin button.

Drawings are saved per symbol and come back when you reopen it. A trend line, ray or horizontal line can also be used as an alert.

Catalyst markers India

Company events show as coloured dots above the candle on the event's date. They come from two places:

  • Automatically from NSE, for every NSE stock: order wins, results dates, JVs and agreements, M&A and restructuring, splits and bonuses, demergers, dividends, buybacks, fund raising, capacity and commissioning, credit rating upgrades and downgrades, regulatory and legal actions, and press releases. Routine paperwork is left out. Click a dot to open the actual NSE filing (a PDF) in a new tab. BSE-only stocks don't get these, because BSE blocks automatic access.
  • Analyst calls, from news headlines, shown as diamonds: green for Buy / Accumulate / Outperform / upgrade, amber for Hold / Neutral / Equal weight, red for Sell / Reduce / downgrade, grey when only a target is mentioned. Hover to see the broker, rating and target (for example "Motilal Oswal: Buy, target ₹488") with the original headline underneath, so you can check what was read; click to open the article. A headline only counts when it names a known brokerage and gives a rating, a target or a target change. Lists of several stocks and generic "price target" forecast sites are skipped. These come from news reports of broker notes, not the brokers' reports themselves, so check the article before acting on one.
  • Your own catalyst file (below), with your themes, scores and conviction. When your file and NSE both have an order win on the same day, yours is shown.
  • Colour = the event's theme. Each theme always has the same colour, on every chart.
  • Hover over a dot to read "Theme: title", with the date, company, category, sentiment, score and conviction underneath. If several events fall on one day, you see them all.
  • Round dot = positive or neutral news. Square = negative news.
  • Dots sit a clear gap above the candle's high, so the top of the wick stays easy to see and click when drawing trend lines or Fibonacci levels.
  • Dots show on daily, weekly and monthly charts only. Intraday charts stay clean.

Choosing which markers show

In Settings, the Chart markers section has a tick box for each kind: your catalyst file, analyst calls, order wins, results, JVs, M&A, splits and bonuses, demergers, dividends, buybacks, fund raising, capacity, credit ratings, regulatory, and news. Untick what you don't want, or use All / None, then Save. The choice is remembered.

Each tick box shows what its marker looks like: its shape and colour. A line above them is the key: ● circle = company event, ■ square = negative news (fraud, default, penalty), ◆ diamond = analyst call (green Buy, amber Hold, red Sell, grey target only).

Watchlists

  • Type a symbol in Add symbol and press Enter. Click a row to open its chart.
  • + List makes a new list; the drop-down switches lists; Delete removes the current list.
  • Broker portfolios are their own watchlists: the list menu has My lists (yours) and Broker portfolios, with one list per connected account, for example "Zerodha portfolio (22)" or "Kotak Neo portfolio (1)". They follow your actual holdings, so they can't be edited or deleted here. Every account connected to the app gets its own list.

Alerts

Click Create alert in the toolbar. An alert can be:

  • Up to five price or indicator conditions, all of them or any of them (for example: close crosses above the 50 EMA and RSI is above 50).
  • Price crossing a trend line, ray or horizontal line you've drawn.

Alerts are checked every 15 seconds on your computer, while the app is running, even with the browser closed. You get a desktop pop-up and a sound, and optionally a message to Discord, Slack or Telegram. The Alerts tab lists them and what has fired.

Screener India

Opens on India (Nifty 500). Also: crypto, US stocks and US ETFs.

  • Filters: search by name, price range, % change range, minimum volume, minimum market cap, and sector (NSE's industry groups).
  • Sort by clicking any column heading.
  • Technical filter: choose an Indicator, adjust its settings (for example Bollinger Bands length 20, StdDev 2), then pick a Condition made for that indicator, such as "Upper above Price by 0% to 5% — Upper band proximity", "Lower crosses down Price — Oversold re-entry", "Crosses up EMA 200 — Golden cross", "MACD crosses up Signal below 0" or "Volume above its MA by 100% or more". Choose the timeframe in On, then Scan top 100 to check the 100 most traded stocks that pass your other filters. Manual setup… at the bottom of the list lets you build any rule yourself.
  • Click a row to open its chart.

How the conditions read

Conditions are worded the way TradingView's screener words them: the indicator line first, then where it is compared with price. "Upper below Price" means the upper band is below the price, so price has broken out above it. "Basis crosses down Price" means price has just crossed above the middle line. The text after the dash says what it means for the trade. "By 0% to 5%" means the gap between the two is between 0% and 5% of the second one. Conditions that list two things ("Crosses up Signal below 0") need both to be true.

Market cap is the live price × shares outstanding. Share counts are refreshed once a week in the background.

Strategy tester and quant checks

Test a trading idea on past prices of the active chart.

  1. Open Strategy tester. Load a ready-made strategy (for example EMA cross 9/21) or build your own entry and exit rules.
  2. Set fees and slippage, and optionally a stop loss, take profit or trailing stop, long or short.

Quant checks

A good backtest can be luck. Quant checks run the tests professional desks use before trusting a result: walk-forward testing, overfitting (PBO), deflated Sharpe, higher costs, Monte Carlo, and nearby settings. You get a pass count and a plain-language verdict.

Your own data

The tester's Your own data row imports a CSV of bars, or a CSV of signals your own code produced, so you can test them the same way.

Bar replay

Practise reading charts without knowing what comes next.

  1. Click Bar replay in the toolbar and click the bar to start from. Everything after it is hidden.
  2. Step forward one bar at a time, or play at 0.5× to 10×, on one chart or every chart in the layout.
  3. Paper trade while it plays. Replay trades use a separate practice account.

Catalyst markers appear only once replay reaches their date.

Paper trading

Practise with pretend money. The Paper trading tab has an order ticket for the active chart (market, limit and stop orders, with an optional stop and target), and shows your positions, orders and profit and loss. Nothing here touches a real account.

Broker accounts: Zerodha, Kotak Neo, ICICI Direct India

Real moneyOrders from the Broker tab go to your real broker account. Every order shows a summary for you to confirm first. Test with 1 share at a limit price far from the market before trading normally.

What you get

  • One button per account in the Broker tab (a ● means logged in today).
  • Available and used funds under the order window, and four tabs on the right, like Zerodha's:
    • Portfolio: holdings (quantity, average price, last price, day change, value, P&L).
    • Orders: today's orders, split into Open (still waiting at the broker) and Executed and closed (filled, cancelled or rejected, with the reason). The tab shows how many are open.
    • Positions: today's positions with their live price (LTP) and P&L.
    • GTT orders: the GTTs Quant Terminal is watching (see GTT orders).
    The app remembers the tab you were on, and after you place an order (or save a GTT) it opens the Orders (or GTT orders) tab. Click a stock in any tab to open its chart.
  • Where a broker doesn't send a figure (Kotak's day change, open-position P&L, ICICI's prices), the app fills it from its live quote.
  • A watchlist per account made from its holdings (see Watchlists).
  • An order ticket for the active chart's NSE stock: Buy or Sell, quantity, Limit / Market / Stop-loss limit / Stop-loss market, Delivery (CNC) or Intraday (MIS). ICICI Direct accounts only offer Limit / Stop-loss limit and Delivery, as ICICI's API requires.
  • After you send an order, the app waits a moment, re-reads the broker's order book and tells you the result: accepted, or rejected with the broker's reason (for example "RMS: Margin Exceeds").

Several accounts, several brokers

The app supports any number of Zerodha, Kotak Neo and ICICI Direct accounts. Each account has its own button in the Broker tab, its own "… portfolio" section in the Watchlist and its own order ticket. Settings → Broker accounts lists them all with their status.

Accounts live in quant-terminal\data\brokers.json, under "accounts". Each has a fixed "id" (used in redirect URLs), a "broker" (zerodha, kotak or icici), a "name" you can change freely (for example "Zerodha – Mom"), and that broker's keys. To add another account, copy an entry, give it a new id such as zerodha-3, and fill in its keys. Type the keys in yourself; they stay on this computer and only go to their own broker.

One-time setup per account

Zerodha (Kite Connect)

  1. Log in to developers.kite.trade with that Zerodha account and create an app. Each Kite app only works with the one Zerodha client ID it belongs to; logging in with another ID gives "The user is not enabled for the app".
  2. Set its Redirect URL to http://127.0.0.1:3777/api/broker/kite/callback (the same for every Zerodha account).
  3. Put its API key and secret in "apiKey" and "apiSecret" of that account.

Kotak Neo

  1. Register for TOTP once at kotaksecurities.com/platform/kotak-neo-trade-api (Google or Microsoft Authenticator).
  2. In Neo, More → Trade API: generate an application, copy its token (the consumer key), and whitelist this computer's IP on it. Kotak refuses API orders ("unauthorized") from any other IP. The Broker tab shows the IP Kotak sees.
  3. Put the token, mobile number (with +91) and client code in "accessToken", "mobileNumber" and "ucc".

ICICI Direct (Breeze API)

  1. Log in to api.icicidirect.com with that ICICI Direct account and register an app.
  2. Set its Redirect URL to http://127.0.0.1:3777/api/broker/id/callback with the account's id, for example .../api/broker/icici-1/callback, and register your static IP. ICICI only accepts API orders from it, and lets you change it once a week.
  3. Put the App Key and Secret Key in "apiKey" and "apiSecret".
ICICI's rules for API ordersLimit and stop-loss limit orders only (SEBI doesn't allow market orders through Breeze), delivery (CNC) only, NSE only. ICICI's stock codes differ from NSE symbols (HDFBAN is HDFCBANK, NIFJUN is JUNIORBEES), so the app matches holdings by ISIN using ICICI's own security list. Unlisted holdings are shown without a chart.

The order window

The left side of the Broker tab is an order window for the active chart's stock, laid out like Zerodha's:

  • Header: the stock, a Buy / Sell switch (blue for buy, orange for sell), and the live NSE and BSE prices. Orders go to NSE; BSE isn't supported yet.
  • Regular tab: Intraday (MIS) or Longterm (CNC), Qty, Price (starts at the live price) and Trigger price, and Market / Limit / SL / SL-M. Fields that don't apply are greyed out.
  • GTT tab: see GTT orders below.
  • Footer: the amount Required (for intraday, about a fifth, as brokers give roughly 5× leverage) and your Available funds, with the Buy / Sell (or Place GTT) button and Cancel to clear the form.

Every order shows a summary to confirm before it is sent. ICICI Direct accounts only offer Limit / SL and Longterm, as ICICI's API requires.

GTT orders and trailing stop-loss (kept in Quant Terminal)

A GTT ("good till triggered") waits for a price before placing an order. Unlike the broker's own GTT, these are kept inside Quant Terminal: nothing is sent to the broker until a trigger is hit, and they work the same for every broker and account.

  • Single: one trigger. Place an order when the price falls to a level (a stop-loss, or buying a dip) or rises to it (a breakout).
  • OCO (one cancels the other): a stop-loss and a target together. Whichever is hit first places its order and the other is cancelled. For a sell, the stop is below the price and the target above.
  • Trailing stop-loss: tick Trailing on the stop-loss and choose how far it follows, as a % or in ₹ from the best price. The stop moves up as the price makes new highs (down as it makes new lows, for a buy) and never moves back. When the price falls back to the stop, the order is placed. If the trail is tighter than the trigger you typed, the stop jumps to the trail at the first check.
  • Each leg places a Limit order (with a trailing stop, the limit price moves with it) or a Market order. ICICI takes no market orders, so a Market leg on an ICICI account goes out as a limit order 1% through the price.
  • % of LTP and the trigger price stay in step: type either one. Triggers start 5% either side of the price.

How it watches: every 5 seconds from 09:15 to 15:30 IST on weekdays, using the same live prices as the charts. When a trigger is hit, Quant Terminal places the order with the GTT's account, then alerts you with a pop-up, a sound and a desktop notification. The GTT orders tab on the right of the Broker panel shows each GTT's triggers (with a trailing stop's current level and best price), the last price and its status.

Prices for Indian stocks can lag a little, so a fast move can go past a trigger before the order is placed. GTT orders are real orders: the same ₹5 lakh limit and broker rules apply.

Logging in each day

Brokers end API sessions overnight (around 6 am), so log in once each trading day, for each account, from the Broker tab:

  • Zerodha and ICICI Direct: click Log in to …. The broker's own login page opens in a pop-up, which closes itself when you're done.
  • Kotak Neo: type your 6-digit TOTP and your MPIN, then Log in to Kotak Neo. Neither is saved.

Safety limits

  • Only NSE stocks (symbols ending in .NS) can be ordered.
  • Any order worth more than ₹5,00,000 is refused. Change "maxOrderValue" in brokers.json to raise or lower this.
  • Market orders need a current price to check the size; if one isn't available, use a limit order.
  • Only this computer can use the Broker features, and other websites can't send orders through the app.
  • Kotak and ICICI only accept API orders from an IP address registered with them. Reading your account works without it.
  • Every order attempt and the broker's reply are written to the app's log (data/server.log), without any keys or tokens.

Indicator editor

Troubleshooting

The page won't open

The app isn't running, usually after a restart. Double-click start.bat, or run node scripts/launch.js from the quant-terminal folder.

A chart says "Couldn't load" or stays empty

  • Check the symbol: Indian stocks need .NS or .BO.
  • Yahoo sometimes limits requests for a few minutes. Wait and reload; the Data status button shows which sources are working.
  • Run node scripts/check.js to test everything.

A chart is squashed, scrolled away or shows no candles

Right-click on it and choose Reset chart view.

No catalyst dots on a stock

  • Switch to a daily or longer timeframe; dots don't show on intraday charts.
  • Check the stock is in catalysts.csv and its date is within the bars loaded.
  • If the company was matched to the wrong code, add a symbol column or fix catalyst-symbols.json.

The heatmap says market caps are still loading

The first time, share counts take a minute or two to load; until then tiles are sized by traded value. Reopen the heatmap shortly.

Broker says "session has ended"

Log in again from the Broker tab. Brokers end sessions overnight.

Zerodha: "The user is not enabled for the app"

You logged in with a Zerodha ID that isn't linked to that account's Kite app. Each Kite app only works for the client ID it was created under: log in with that ID, or create the app under the other account on developers.kite.trade.

Zerodha or ICICI: the pop-up says "127.0.0.1 refused to connect"

The Redirect URL in the broker's app settings is missing the port or path. It must be exactly http://127.0.0.1:3777/api/broker/kite/callback for Zerodha, or http://127.0.0.1:3777/api/broker/icici-1/callback (with that account's id) for ICICI. To finish the login you're in, add :3777 after 127.0.0.1 in the pop-up's address and press Enter.

Orders refused: "unauthorized" (Kotak, ICICI) or "No IPs configured" (Zerodha)

The order came from an IP address that isn't registered. The Broker tab shows the IP Kotak sees; whitelist it on the account's Trade API application in Neo (More → Trade API), log in again, then retry. For Zerodha ("No IPs configured for this app"), add the IP under IP Whitelist on your Kite Connect Profile page (developers.kite.trade → Profile, up to 2 IPs) and save; the Redirect URL stays the same. For ICICI, register your static IP on the Breeze API app. Home internet IPs can change; if orders stop working later, check the IP again or ask your provider for a static IP.

Kotak: "Invalid field 'MobileNumber'"

Write the mobile number with the country code, for example +919876543210, in brokers.json.

An order was refused

The message says why: over your ₹5 lakh limit, not an NSE stock, missing price or trigger, or the broker's own reason, such as "RMS: Margin Exceeds" when the order needs more funds than the account has available (shown under Funds). Check the Orders tab for the broker's status message.
